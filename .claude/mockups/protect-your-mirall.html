The one idea users learn: “Your backup folder holds a copy of your spaces and your recovery key. Your passphrase opens it. Keep a second copy of the key somewhere else.” New copy avoids “identity”, “master secret” and “snapshot”; shipped screens that still say “identity” (Restore screen, locked screen) are a follow-up copy pass.

Prompt rules (so it helps, never nags)

  • At most one backup prompt on screen, and at most one per week of any kind.
  • Offer card: new users after the first space (or 7 days after install, whichever is first); existing users on the first launch after the update if they have a space. “Not now” → 30 days, then 60; three times at most.
  • Devices without a system keychain (“weak” protection) see the offer on day one, worded for it: the backup and key are their real protection.
  • Passphrase check: 14 days after setup, then every 6 months; one 1-week snooze; opt-out for password-manager users.
  • Stale backup: one toast at 10 days without a backup; Settings row says “Needs attention” until it recovers. Nothing on success.
  • Never modal, never blocking, never in onboarding.

Rejected

  • Make setup part of onboarding. Nothing to protect yet; keys created then are the ones people lose (Element #15416). Restore stays in onboarding; setup does not.
  • A launch modal for existing users. Dismissed on reflex; What's New stays text and the card does the asking.
  • An “I wrote it down” checkbox. Self-attestation catches nothing; Mirall checks the saved key itself.
  • Keep the key only on this computer. It dies with the computer — the one case it exists for.
  • Separate “Back up your recovery key” and “Backup” features. Two places, two mental models, and a backup that can exist without a key.
  • A notification for every backup. Trains people to ignore the one that matters.

Decisions this needs

  1. The recovery key lives in the backup folder (passphrase-protected), with a second copy recommended — vs. always separate.
  2. Where prompt state lives (“snoozed until”, “last check”): it is not a preference, so not config.json — a small worker-side record.
  3. Settings gets a Backup & recovery entry; Storage loses its Backup section; Profile’s recovery-key row links to it.
  4. Later phase: offer backup when an external drive is plugged in (“Use “Backup” for Mirall?”), and a printable recovery sheet.
